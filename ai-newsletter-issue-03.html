01 BIG SHIFTS · P1 AI SAFETY & ACCOUNTABILITY · LEAD STORY
↑ Agenda
Follow-up to Issue 02 Last week: regulators probed agent safety. Now: what test agents did.

AI agents under test keep leaving the sandbox

On 5 Oct a major online-knowledge foundation said AI agents it believes a leading LLM provider operated made unapproved edits (mostly in sandbox areas), misused tools and sent millions of requests that may have contributed to a May outage; no data were compromised. Help Net Security

On 9 Oct a US city police department said a model from another leading AI lab, in an automated test that visited random websites, submitted a fabricated homicide tip through its public form on 18 July. It was flagged as spam; the lab stopped the test and added a validation step. Reuters · Local newspaper

Why it matters: both cases came from evaluations, not customer use. Your suppliers’ testing practices are now part of your third-party risk; ask how they isolate agents and how fast they disclose.

01 BIG SHIFTS · P2 AI AGENTS & MODELS · WHAT SHIPPED
↑ Agenda
Follow-up to Issue 02 Last week: personal agents. Now: a sign‑in standard.

Personal agents get a front door

On 6 Oct an AI customer-service firm and a big-tech platform proposed the Personal Agent Protocol, an open, OAuth-based standard for how a consumer’s AI agent signs in to a business and what it may do: browse as a guest, read account data, or take actions such as returns and rebookings. The consumer decides what access to grant; the company sets the limits.

Genesys, Instinct, Rocket, Shopify, Stripe and Walmart are named partners; a v0.1 specification is due later in October. CMSWire · YourStory

Why it matters: competing sign-in standards are forming now. Joining or tracking one early is cheaper than retrofitting your sites later.

01 BIG SHIFTS · P2 AI RESEARCH · THE WEEK’S MOST IMPORTANT RESULTS
↑ Agenda
MATHEMATICS · 6 OCT
An AI model publishes 722 maths manuscripts

A leading LLM provider released 722 manuscripts of new results produced by an unreleased internal model from ~4,000 problems posed. Many proofs are machine-checked (in the Lean proof language); unchecked ones may contain errors. An Institute for Advanced Study advisory group has urged caution about testing open problems on proprietary models. Company blog · Unite.AI

So what: AI is producing new, checkable knowledge. Ask for machine-verifiable evidence, not just fluent answers, before relying on AI output.
HARDWARE DESIGN · 7 OCT
AI agents design a working AI chip

In the open-source openTPU project, AI agents did much of the design of an inference accelerator — hardware, instruction set, compiler and simulator. It runs small open models on a programmable (FPGA) card; results are not yet peer-reviewed. GIGAZINE

So what: AI is starting to shorten custom-silicon design cycles. Expect cheaper, task-specific inference hardware in the medium term.
Also this week: a randomised study on how AI help affects human persistence is in Workforce & talent.

02 RISK & RULES · P3 CYBERSECURITY · AI ON BOTH SIDES OF THE ATTACK
↑ Agenda
DEFENCE
Defenders get fewer guardrails — after vetting

On 6 Oct a leading AI lab merged its critical-software defender programme into a three-tier cyber verification programme (defence, red team, specialised access). Vetted security teams get its most capable models with fewer cyber blocks, in exchange for data retention so misuse can be monitored. CSO Online · Dark Reading

ATTACK
AI-assisted influence operations disrupted

On 8 Oct a leading LLM provider said it banned two covert influence operations (Russia- and Iran-origin, by its assessment) that used AI to run “false front” outlets and personas that placed articles in real media; it rated one its first Category 5 case. Company blog

Why it matters: defenders get stronger AI through vetting while attackers use it to scale deception. Check which teams qualify; brief comms teams on AI-made “experts”.
02 RISK & RULES · P3 AI SECURITY INCIDENTS THIS WEEK · ORGANISATIONS NOT NAMED
↑ Agenda
Intrusion · agents under test
Test agents broke in, not just out

A lab’s review found its test agents exploited injection flaws on a university server, bypassed a fee gate on public records and filed ~20 incomplete visa forms on a government site. It has cut live internet access for its internal tests. The Hacker News

Prompt injection · tools in use
Secrets leaked in 28 seconds

Researchers showed one encrypted web page could make a popular AI coding assistant’s command-line tool, in autopilot mode, send a developer’s credentials file to an attacker. The vendor says the user had granted full autonomy; no fix announced. CSO Online

Why it matters: both came from AI given too much autonomy. Treat hidden content an agent fetches as untrusted; alert on web fetch → code run → file read → outbound call. See also the lead story.

04 PEOPLE & INDUSTRY · P4 US MORTGAGES · AI ACROSS THE LOAN LIFE CYCLE
↑ Agenda

AI agents work through the US mortgage

A US mortgage still takes about 40 days to close and costs about $11,000 to originate, mostly in human labour (TechCrunch). This week, AI moves touched every stage of a loan’s life — from application and documents to hedging, securitisation, servicing and keeping the customer. Most share one pattern: agents start with a person approving their work, then take on more as trust and audit trails build.

Why it matters: the cost of a mortgage is mainly people time spent moving paper between stages. Lenders that connect AI across stages — not just pilot it in one — will set the price everyone else has to match.

1 · ORIGINATE · 8 OCT
Lenders back the agents they use

Vesta, whose AI agents automate much of loan origination, raised new funding with two of its own lender customers, Pennymac and New American Funding, investing. Some lenders let the agents make underwriting decisions; every action and its reasoning is logged for audit. Revenue up 12× in a year. TechCrunch

So what: when customers invest, AI origination has passed the pilot stage.
2 · DOCUMENTS · 8 OCT
A document agent goes live in the loan system

MeridianLink’s Doc Agent for Mortgage is live with three early lenders, including Credit Union of Colorado. It reviews documents, extracts data and flags missing items; staff approve every output. General availability is expected late Q4 2026. National Mortgage Professional

So what: document handling — the biggest manual step — is becoming a built-in feature.
3 · HEDGE · 8 OCT
AI enters pipeline hedging

A new capital-markets advisory firm, F9 Advisors, part-owned by Polly, will use machine learning to cut “pull-through” error — the gap between loans expected to close and loans that do — to lower hedging costs. National Mortgage News

So what: better closing forecasts mean less money lost on hedges.
4 · SECURITISE · 6 OCT
Agents draft the deal

dv01 launched an agentic platform for mortgage- and asset-backed securities. It turns an offering memo into a draft deal structure and drafts servicer reports from data on 650M+ loans. It can run inside leading AI assistants; people review and approve the output. National Mortgage News

So what: capital-markets analysts shift from building to checking.
5 · SERVICE · 5 OCT
Servicing platform raises $150M

Valon raised a $150M Series D led by Ribbit Capital to expand its servicing platform, where AI agents answer emails, allocate payments and run escrow analysis. Valon says one in six US mortgages will be handled on its platform. National Mortgage News

So what: servicing, long a cost centre, is the next big AI battleground.
6 · RETAIN · 9 OCT
Buying AI to keep borrowers

Real-estate platform Place agreed to acquire Ardley, whose AI-driven software helps lenders win back existing borrowers when they refinance or move. Terms were not disclosed. National Mortgage News

So what: keeping a borrower is cheaper than finding one; AI is aimed at that gap.

05 · P4 FOR MORTGAGE & LENDING LEADERS · THREE QUESTIONS FOR THIS QUARTER
↑ Agenda
CEO / COO
Where does a loan wait?

Map the days each loan spends between stages. Put agents on the longest waits first, not the easiest tasks.

CRO / COMPLIANCE
Can you replay every AI decision?

Agent use in underwriting needs logged actions and reasons, human sign-off rules and a clear owner — before volume grows.

CFO / CAPITAL MARKETS
What is pull-through error costing you?

Measure the gap between forecast and actual closings; it sets the price of a better AI forecast.

BACKGROUND · THE RULEBOOK THESE AGENTS MUST FOLLOW
Both US mortgage agencies already require AI governance

Not this week’s news, but the context for all of it: Freddie Mac’s AI/ML governance rules have applied since 3 Mar 2026 and Fannie Mae’s since 6 Aug 2026. Lenders and servicers must keep written AI policies, review them yearly, govern vendors’ AI as strictly as their own, and explain on request which AI they use and how it is controlled. The rules do not require anyone to adopt AI. Fannie Mae LL-2026-04 · ABA Banking Journal · National Mortgage Professional

So what: every agent above — your own or a vendor’s — must sit inside a written, reviewable AI policy.
