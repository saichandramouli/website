PREPRINT · 28 OCT 2026 · AGENT RELIABILITY
Preprint: a “decision gate” checks what an AI agent is about to do before it acts
The authors' framework, JevAct, turns domain policies into checks that run before selected agent actions; a separate decision model
scores the structured questions as probabilities, and flagged actions get targeted feedback for a bounded revision. No change to the
agent's model or tools is needed. Tested with eleven LLM agents on τ²-bench Airline and Telecom tasks and a function-calling
benchmark (BFCL), the authors report reliability gains, most consistent on telecom and BFCL; airline results were “more mixed”.
Authors' own measurements; not peer reviewed.
Shoham I, David Y, Akrabi O, Bilgory E, Levy I, Marreed S, Zeltyn S, Shlomov S. “Think Before You Act: Decision Gate for LLM Agents.” arXiv preprint v1,
28 Oct 2026. arxiv.org/abs/2610.38620v1R10 · DOI 10.48550/arXiv.2610.38620 (arXiv-issued)
BUSINESS LENS · FROM THE SOURCE
The tests use τ²-bench Airline and Telecom tasks and BFCL. The
authors report gains were not uniform: airline results were “more mixed”.

PREPRINT (MEDRXIV) · V2 POSTED 28 OCT 2026 · HEALTHCARE
QUALITY ASSURANCE
Preprint: AI models caught most planted errors in
clinical-trial reports, and flagged issues in reports
without them
The authors inserted methodological errors into randomised clinical-trial
reports and asked 15 LLMs (four open-weight) to review them. On 58 trials
published in 2026, major-error detection ranged from 58% to 99%, with
mean review times of 1.4 to 16.3 minutes. Models “still flagged 14% to 29%
of the error types in control reports without inserted errors”.
Limits: the human comparison (27% mean detection) comes from an earlier
study of three reports; scoring used two blinded LLM judges; four authors
are affiliated with ResearchHub Foundation, “whose open-science platform
… is implementing AI-assisted review”. Not peer reviewed.
Brian D, Pereira Hernández XI, Yin H, Koury J, Rust R. “Large language models detect
methodological errors that human peer review misses in clinical trials.” medRxiv
preprint v2, posted 28 Oct 2026; v1 date not confirmed.

CLOUD AI SERVICE · REPORT DATED 26 OCT 2026 · PROVIDER'S OWN ACCOUNT
A large cloud provider says 155 responses from one hosted model contained other users'
data
In the provider's incident report, a hosted coding model returned 155 responses containing data from other users' sessions between
27 Aug and 9 Sep 2026, all to very long prompts. The model was offered with a larger context window than its cache could hold; the
provider says, “We were aware of this mismatch before launch.” A bug in the open-source SGLang engine then let those requests
read leftover data from earlier requests. A researcher reported it on 8 Sep; the provider made the model private within about 40
minutes of declaring an incident, found “no evidence of deliberate exploitation”, and says no other hosted model was affected. The
provider is not named because the news is negative.
Source: provider's incident reportR5
