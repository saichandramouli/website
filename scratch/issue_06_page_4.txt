PREPRINT · 28 OCT 2026 · PROMPT INJECTION
Preprint: where a hidden instruction sits can change whether AI agents obey it
The authors built three agent testbeds (tool use, coding and daily workflows) and moved the same injected instruction between
locations. They report that agents trust some parts of their environment more than others, which they call the “trust region effect”. In
one result, an agent powered by OpenAI's GPT-5.4 followed an unsafe instruction placed in a repository documentation file (README
or TODO) 2.6 times more often on average than the same instruction placed in a code or data file. Not peer reviewed.
Guo C, Liu X, Yang W, Feng M, Song J, Ma Q, Buractaon C, White C, Li B, Gao J. “Where You Inject Matters: The Trust Region Effect in LLM Agents.” arXiv
preprint v1, 28 Oct 2026. arxiv.org/abs/2610.38867R8 · DOI 10.48550/arXiv.2610.38867

INDIA · LAUNCHED IN BENGALURU, REPORTED 31 OCT 2026 · DRAFT FRAMEWORK
MeitY releases a draft handbook for training AI on sensitive data without handing it over
Status: marked “Draft for Discussion”; a framework, not a law. Scope: the handbook on the Data Empowerment and Protection
Architecture (DEPA) for AI Training, prepared with the iSPIRT Foundation and steered by MeitY, proposes that training happens where
the data sits: in its main design, raw data stays with the training-data provider inside secure data environments and is not exposed to
those using it for training. It names roles including training-data providers and consumers and data-exchange platforms. It calls itself
a “living resource”, invites “actionable recommendations” and gives no deadline. As reported by ETV Bharat, MeitY Secretary S
Krishnan unveiled it at a roundtable.
Sources: MeitY handbook (PDF)R12 · ETV BharatR12a

EUROPE · ROMANIA · 30 OCT 2026 · COMPANY PLANS
Runware plans an assembly line for factory-built AI data-centre pods
As reported by TechCrunch, Runware's chief executive says the company plans to scale its Romania factory to 30 MW of data-centre
capacity per day by 2028 (“nearly 1 gigawatt per month”), with a second factory due early next year and US expansion to follow. The
liquid-cooled pods need power and internet but no water connection. Runware aims to have pods online within 48 hours of delivery
(installation and setup, not manufacturing) and cites $1 billion of committed interest covering more than 20 MW. Company plans and
claims, not measured output.
Source: TechCrunch (Dominic-Madori Davis)R6
