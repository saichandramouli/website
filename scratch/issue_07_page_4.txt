UNITED STATES · 4 NOV 2026 · SUPERVISORY FRAMEWORK, NOT A RULE
US bank regulator issues its ﬁrst AI framework for examiners — and declines to treat AI as a risk
apart
The Office of the Comptroller of the Currency, which supervises national banks and federal savings associations, issued Bulletin 202654 and a
supervisory framework for examiners evaluating banks' use of AI. Note the terminology before searching for it: the document calls the technology “super
intelligenceˮ, or SI. Examiners will apply it to SI in normal-course activities including credit decisioning, payments, fraud detection, customer service,
operations, risk management and market activities, with particular attention to generative and agentic systems; it sets out 16 supervisory components
across six pillars and is described as a living document. Two points matter for a board. Examiners are told not to treat SI as a separate and distinct risk
category, and banks need not define a separate SI risk appetite  SI risk belongs inside existing operational, compliance and credit frameworks. And
scrutiny follows what a system is permitted to do and the consequences if it fails, not how sophisticated the model is. Status: the framework sets no new
supervisory expectations or enforceable standards; Reuters notes it is not legally binding in the way a rule is, though banks typically comply closely with
such guidance.
Sources: OCC Bulletin 202654 and news release NR 202697, 4 Nov 2026 · ABA Banking Journal · Reuters Pete Schroeder) · Reed Smith analysis

INDUSTRY RESEARCH · 5 NOV 2026 · PARIS · FINANCIAL SERVICES
Still unsolved: proving that agentic AI paid for itself
The Capgemini Research Institute's World Cloud Report for Financial Services 2027, published 5 November, draws on a survey of 1,100 financial services
leaders across 14 markets, fielded June to July 2026, in seven sectors from retail banking to health insurance. It reports that 59% of firms are piloting or
deploying multi-agent AI systems, yet only 10% have achieved enterprise-wide orchestration and only 5% have institutionalised the capabilities to run
agentic AI at enterprise scale. Roughly half of firms cannot consistently demonstrate a return on agentic AI initiatives. On cost, 90% are using or planning
to implement intelligent model routing to optimise performance and manage token costs, and 52% are turning to hyperscalers as their preferred cloud
partner to govern multi-agent deployments. The release ties the gap to measurement rather than ambition: firms lack clear frameworks for judging
whether initiatives deliver enough value to justify continued investment. It reports eight in ten executives prioritising reduction of technical debt, calls a
modernised tech stack a prerequisite for the agentic era, and projects that executives making outcomes their primary AI-funding criterion will rise 141%
by 2028 while those focused on efficiency fall 42%. Ravi Khokhar, Capgemini's Global Head of Cloud for Financial Services, is quoted on p6.)
Source: Capgemini press release and World Cloud Report for Financial Services 2027, 5 Nov 2026. Survey figures are the publisher's own; projections to 2028 are the report's
forecast. Capgemini sells cloud and AI services of the kind the report finds firms need.

04 CAPITAL & MARKETS · P4 INFRASTRUCTURE & CAPACITY ↑ Agenda
UNITED STATES · WYOMING · 5 NOV 2026 · COMPANY ANNOUNCEMENT
Google puts $17 billion behind a ﬁrst data centre in Wyoming, with the power contracted
alongside it
In a post by Steve Walter, vice president of global infrastructure and energy, Google said it is building its first data centre in Wyoming, sited in Laramie
County and “making a long-term, $17 billion investment in the Cowboy Stateˮ. The design is air-cooled, which the company ties to limiting demands on
local water. The power is contracted rather than self-built: the project is contracted with AEP for a commercial-scale 702-megawatt fuel-cell
development, with the cells made by Bloom Energy, while gas generators run by Tallgrass Energy and Black Hills Energy also supply power. Carbon
capture sits with that gas generation, not the fuel cells  Tallgrass is developing a nearby sequestration hub. The announcement gives no construction
schedule, and the 702 MW figure is a power figure, not the data centre's capacity. For buyers of compute, the detail to note is the structure rather than
the headline number: this announcement pairs the capacity with the power arrangements meant to run it.
Source: Google blog post, 5 Nov 2026. Company plans; not measured output. Our summary: here, capacity and power are announced together.
