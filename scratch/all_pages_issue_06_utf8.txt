PREPRINT · 28 OCT 2026 · AGENT RELIABILITY
Preprint: a “decision gate” checks what an AI agent is about to do before it acts
The authors' framework, JevAct, turns domain policies into checks that run before selected agent actions; a separate decision model
scores the structured questions as probabilities, and flagged actions get targeted feedback for a bounded revision. No change to the
agent's model or tools is needed. Tested with eleven LLM agents on τ²-bench Airline and Telecom tasks and a function-calling
benchmark (BFCL), the authors report reliability gains, most consistent on telecom and BFCL; airline results were “more mixed”.
Authors' own measurements; not peer reviewed.
Shoham I, David Y, Akrabi O, Bilgory E, Levy I, Marreed S, Zeltyn S, Shlomov S. “Think Before You Act: Decision Gate for LLM Agents.” arXiv preprint v1,
28 Oct 2026. arxiv.org/abs/2610.38620v1R10 · DOI 10.48550/arXiv.2610.38620 (arXiv-issued)
BUSINESS LENS · FROM THE SOURCE
The tests use τ²-bench Airline and Telecom tasks and BFCL. The
authors report gains were not uniform: airline results were “more mixed”.

PREPRINT (MEDRXIV) · V2 POSTED 28 OCT 2026 · HEALTHCARE
QUALITY ASSURANCE
Preprint: AI models caught most planted errors in
clinical-trial reports, and flagged issues in reports
without them
The authors inserted methodological errors into randomised clinical-trial
reports and asked 15 LLMs (four open-weight) to review them. On 58 trials
published in 2026, major-error detection ranged from 58% to 99%, with
mean review times of 1.4 to 16.3 minutes. Models “still flagged 14% to 29%
of the error types in control reports without inserted errors”.
Limits: the human comparison (27% mean detection) comes from an earlier
study of three reports; scoring used two blinded LLM judges; four authors
are affiliated with ResearchHub Foundation, “whose open-science platform
… is implementing AI-assisted review”. Not peer reviewed.
Brian D, Pereira Hernández XI, Yin H, Koury J, Rust R. “Large language models detect
methodological errors that human peer review misses in clinical trials.” medRxiv
preprint v2, posted 28 Oct 2026; v1 date not confirmed.
doi.org/10.64898/2026.10.25.26366455R11

CLOUD AI SERVICE · REPORT DATED 26 OCT 2026 · PROVIDER'S OWN ACCOUNT
A large cloud provider says 155 responses from one hosted model contained other users'
data
In the provider's incident report, a hosted coding model returned 155 responses containing data from other users' sessions between
27 Aug and 9 Sep 2026, all to very long prompts. The model was offered with a larger context window than its cache could hold; the
provider says, “We were aware of this mismatch before launch.” A bug in the open-source SGLang engine then let those requests
read leftover data from earlier requests. A researcher reported it on 8 Sep; the provider made the model private within about 40
minutes of declaring an incident, found “no evidence of deliberate exploitation”, and says no other hosted model was affected. The
provider is not named because the news is negative.
Source: provider's incident reportR5
BUSINESS LENS · FROM THE SOURCE
Per the provider, the exposure ran from 27 Aug to 9 Sep, was reported
by a security researcher through its HackerOne programme, and it knew
of the size mismatch before launch.

PREPRINT · 28 OCT 2026 · PROMPT INJECTION
Preprint: where a hidden instruction sits can change whether AI agents obey it
The authors built three agent testbeds (tool use, coding and daily workflows) and moved the same injected instruction between
locations. They report that agents trust some parts of their environment more than others, which they call the “trust region effect”. In
one result, an agent powered by OpenAI's GPT-5.4 followed an unsafe instruction placed in a repository documentation file (README
or TODO) 2.6 times more often on average than the same instruction placed in a code or data file. Not peer reviewed.
Guo C, Liu X, Yang W, Feng M, Song J, Ma Q, Buractaon C, White C, Li B, Gao J. “Where You Inject Matters: The Trust Region Effect in LLM Agents.” arXiv
preprint v1, 28 Oct 2026. arxiv.org/abs/2610.38867R8 · DOI 10.48550/arXiv.2610.38867
BUSINESS LENS · FROM THE SOURCE
In one result, an agent powered by GPT-5.4 followed the same unsafe
instruction 2.6 times more often on average in a documentation file than
in a code or data file.
ASK YOUR LEADERSHIP TEAM · EDITOR'S QUESTION

emails or tickets?

INDIA · LAUNCHED IN BENGALURU, REPORTED 31 OCT 2026 · DRAFT FRAMEWORK
MeitY releases a draft handbook for training AI on sensitive data without handing it over
Status: marked “Draft for Discussion”; a framework, not a law. Scope: the handbook on the Data Empowerment and Protection
Architecture (DEPA) for AI Training, prepared with the iSPIRT Foundation and steered by MeitY, proposes that training happens where
the data sits: in its main design, raw data stays with the training-data provider inside secure data environments and is not exposed to
those using it for training. It names roles including training-data providers and consumers and data-exchange platforms. It calls itself
a “living resource”, invites “actionable recommendations” and gives no deadline. As reported by ETV Bharat, MeitY Secretary S
Krishnan unveiled it at a roundtable.
Sources: MeitY handbook (PDF)R12 · ETV BharatR12a
BUSINESS LENS · FROM THE SOURCE
In the draft's main design, raw data stays with its provider and is not
exposed to those training on it. The draft invites recommendations.
ASK YOUR LEADERSHIP TEAM · EDITOR'S QUESTION

cannot do today, and should we respond to the draft?

EUROPE · ROMANIA · 30 OCT 2026 · COMPANY PLANS
Runware plans an assembly line for factory-built AI data-centre pods
As reported by TechCrunch, Runware's chief executive says the company plans to scale its Romania factory to 30 MW of data-centre
capacity per day by 2028 (“nearly 1 gigawatt per month”), with a second factory due early next year and US expansion to follow. The
liquid-cooled pods need power and internet but no water connection. Runware aims to have pods online within 48 hours of delivery
(installation and setup, not manufacturing) and cites $1 billion of committed interest covering more than 20 MW. Company plans and
claims, not measured output.
Source: TechCrunch (Dominic-Madori Davis)R6
BUSINESS LENS · FROM THE SOURCE

measured output).
